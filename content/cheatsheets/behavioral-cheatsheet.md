## STAR Structure

| Part | What to cover | Time share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | Brief context: where, what, why it mattered | ~10% |
| Task | What you personally owned | ~10% |
| Action | Your steps, choices, and reasons, in order | ~60% |
| Result | Measurable outcome plus what you learned | ~20% |

Rules: use "I" for your actions, quantify honestly, keep answers near two minutes, and end cleanly with outcome plus lesson.

## Common Question Themes

| Theme | What it tests | Story tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leadership | Influence, ownership, decisions | Show a decision you drove, not just a title held |
| Conflict | Listening, judgement, resolution | Describe the disagreement neutrally; end with the relationship intact |
| Failure | Ownership, growth | Pick a real failure; spend most time on the lesson and changed behavior |
| Challenge | Resilience, problem-solving | Name what made it hard, not just what happened |
| Initiative | Self-direction | Show noticing a problem yourself and following through |
| Learning | Adaptability | Show how you structured the learning and what you delivered with it |
| Motivation | Fit, research | Be specific about the work, team, or product — not generic praise |

## Project Presentation Checklist

| Step | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Problem first | Open with what was broken or missing, and for whom — not the tech stack |
| Your role | State plainly what you owned and what others owned |
| The hard part | Name one genuinely difficult problem and your approach |
| Decisions with reasons | Every technology appears as a choice with an alternative considered |
| Honest numbers | Scale and impact figures you can defend under follow-ups |
| Lesson | Close with what you would do differently now |
| Invite depth | Pause and ask what the interviewer wants to go deeper on |

Prepare a two-minute and a ten-minute version of two projects you truly owned.

## Architecture Deep Dive: Top-Down Order

| Layer | Cover |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Purpose and users | What the system does, who uses it, at what scale |
| 2. Components and request flow | Trace one request end to end through the main components |
| 3. Data and storage | What lives where, why that store, how consistency is handled |
| 4. Scale | What saturates first at 10x load, and what you would change |
| 5. Failure | What breaks when each dependency fails, how you detect and recover |
| 6. Evolution | How the design changed, and what the next change would be |

Keep diagrams to five to seven boxes. Every component needs a reason, not just a name.

## Trade-off Template

| Part | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | What did you choose, plainly? |
| Alternatives | What were the real alternatives, fairly stated? |
| Deciding constraint | Deadline, correctness need, team capacity, scale reality? |
| Cost | What did you give up, and how did you manage it? |
| Revisit if | Under what condition would you choose differently? |

A lesson is a specific behavior change caused by a specific experience — not a platitude like "communication is important."

## Traps to Avoid

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Team story with no "I" | Narrate your decisions, not just events |
| Endless background | Cut any detail that does not explain a decision you made |
| No result | Always close with what changed, even if partial |
| Humble-brag failure | Pick a real failure with a real lesson |
| Trade-off with no cost | Name the downside before the interviewer asks |
| Perfect system | Name real weaknesses, incidents, and regrets |

## Revision Summary

- STAR: 20% context, 60% Action, 20% Result; add a Learning ending for failure questions.
- Map questions to stories by competency, from a bank of six to eight stories.
- Present projects as problem, role, hard part, outcome, lesson — in that order.
- Deep dives go top-down and shift from description to reasoning about scale, failure, and evolution.
- Every trade-off needs a decision, alternatives, deciding constraint, cost, and revisit condition.
